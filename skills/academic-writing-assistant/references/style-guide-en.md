# English Academic Style Guide

Prefer clear verbs, identifiable subjects and evidence-bounded claims. More elaborate language does not establish more rigorous science. Preserve the user's target language and requested register; SCI or a publisher name does not define a single house style.

## Common revision patterns

- Reduce nominalizations when meaning is unchanged: `perform an analysis of` → `analyze`.
- Break long connective chains while preserving the actual causal or conditional relation.
- Move the subject and verb closer; retain qualifications where their scope is clear.
- Correct agreement, articles and countability without adding research details.
- Remove genuine repetitions, not meaningful frequency, uncertainty or scope.

A generic opener can be removed or replaced by a problem the author supplied. Do not invent a numerical threshold, a dataset or a failure mode to make it concrete.

## Synthetic example with no added facts

Source: This paper carries out an analysis of noise in the images.

Revision: This paper analyzes noise in the images.

The revision changes grammar and phrasing only. It cannot become “Noise reduces small-object detection accuracy below 0.5 m resolution” without evidence for those additional claims.

## Hedging and precise vocabulary

Interpret hedges in context rather than ordering every verb on one fixed ladder. `can` may express capability while `may` expresses uncertainty. Keep meaningful frequency and scope; avoid stripping all hedges to create a confident tone.

Not automatic synonyms:

| Source | Why the replacement needs review |
|---|---|
| better features → more discriminative features | Adds a particular property of the features |
| many experiments → extensive experiments | May add a claim about breadth of evaluation |
| significant improvement → consistent improvement | Changes statistical/importance language into repeatability |
| performs well → outperforms baselines | Adds a comparative result |
| associated with → leads to | Adds causation |

Specific numeric alternatives require author-supplied values or an authorized, explicitly described calculation. `to our knowledge` does not validate a firstness claim.

## Tense and voice

Use tense for the sentence's function: method description, completed experiment, reported observation, interpretation or actual future plan. Present and past tense can legitimately coexist in a paragraph. Changing a future action into a completed action is a factual L3 change.

First person and passive voice can both be appropriate. Follow the actual venue guide or a neutral register, without blanket assumptions that journals prefer passive voice or conferences permit every informal construction.

## Translation from Chinese

Retain frequency, uncertainty, conditions and comparison scope. Translate the argument rather than preserving every clause boundary, but do not silently delete substantive implications. Ceremonial phrases may be flagged as L3 candidates for removal; replacing them with an actual contribution requires supplied evidence.

New translations do not receive fictional L1 correction counts. Explain important terminology choices in the user's language when useful.
